# Meteor Detector

The Meteor Detector channel is intended for RF meteor-scatter trail monitoring. It channelizes the selected receiver offset and resamples the channel to 100, 300, 1000, or 3000 Hz. The GUI displays the same resampled IQ in two independently configurable waterfalls. The trail-echo view defaults to a 1024-point FFT with 50% overlap for frequency resolution, while the head-echo view defaults to a 128-point FFT with 75% overlap and faster updates for time resolution.

The Geometry rollup records the radar transmitter latitude, longitude, beam azimuth, elevation, azimuth beamwidth, and elevation beamwidth, plus the receive antenna azimuth, elevation, and HPBW. Receive pointing can be entered manually or synchronized once per second from a selected GS232Controller feature. Selecting no rotator restores manual receive azimuth and elevation entry. The current receive pointing and beamwidth are included in exported RMOB reports.

The map button sends translucent transmitter and receiver beam volumes to open Map features, together with their geometric intersection. The orange TX and cyan RX solids are capped at the selected maximum altitude, which defaults to 1000 km. Their brighter green overlap is the region inside both antenna beam envelopes; it is a geometric coverage volume rather than a received-power or radar-sensitivity prediction. The 2D map displays corresponding footprints. The GRAVES defaults use approximately 180 degrees of azimuth beamwidth across the southern sky and 25 degrees of elevation beamwidth. A zero azimuth or elevation beamwidth leaves that antenna pattern unspecified and suppresses its volume.

Detected Doppler sweeps are listed in the Satellites tab. Alongside signed center-Doppler speed, the table reports maximum absolute equivalent radial speed from the fitted sweep endpoints and average Doppler drift rate. An RF-only classification combines endpoint speed, drift rate, total frequency excursion, and track duration into Aircraft, Satellite, or Uncertain, with a heuristic satellite score. The score is not a calibrated probability.

When an ADS-B Demod channel is open, fresh aircraft position, altitude, ground speed, track or heading, and vertical-rate reports are converted to source-neutral moving-target states; the fields must also be sampled close to the position report's own time, and a state whose direction comes from heading rather than measured track is slightly down-weighted in scoring. The matcher extrapolates each state through the sweep interval, predicts the bistatic Doppler at the sweep start and end times from the configured transmitter and receiver positions, and scores the observed center and drift residuals.

Satellite matching uses the same prediction and scoring path. A plugin-local worker thread downloads the CelesTrak active OMM CSV catalog plus supplemental visual, analyst, recent-launch, GEO protected-zone, potential-decay, FENGYUN 1C, IRIDIUM 33, and COSMOS 2251 element sets. The CelesTrak SATCAT CSV supplies canonical names and object types and filters objects already marked as decayed. The Satellites information dialog can also configure a Space-Track login; when configured, the worker authenticates to `space-track.org` and downloads its comprehensive GP 3LE set. The password can be retained in local application settings but is never included in Meteor presets or WebAPI data. All files are cached under the application's `meteor` data directory and refreshed when their cache is older than a day. Public CelesTrak sources use HTTP conditional requests so unchanged files are not re-downloaded and remain a fallback when Space-Track is not configured or unavailable. Overlapping element sets are deduplicated by NORAD catalog ID and the newest epoch wins. SATCAT alone is not propagated because it does not contain a complete orbital state. The OMM CSV and Space-Track 3LE adapters preserve six-digit/Alpha-5 NORAD catalog IDs while converting the orbital fields for the bundled SGP4 propagator. SGP4 propagation, maximum-altitude, horizon, and configured TX/RX beam filtering, and matching all run on that worker. Geometric culling snapshots are shared by detections in the same five-second interval to keep accelerated file replay responsive; the horizon and beam tests are padded by a margin so a fast mover near an edge at the snapshot instant is not lost. Each candidate that survives culling has its Doppler evaluated by SGP4 propagation at the sweep's own start and end times, so orbital curvature is included in the predicted drift rather than extrapolating a single velocity. The information button on the Satellites tab shows loaded object categories and exclusive rejection counts for the latest matching snapshot. Live sources use the detection's system UTC; FileInput and SigMF FileInput use the recording time from the device report, corrected for detection age and accelerated playback. The best ADS-B, TLE, and lunar results are ranked together; a row is identified as `Aircraft (ADS-B)`, `Satellite (TLE)`, or `Moon` only when its score is at least 60 and leads every other candidate by at least 8 points. Otherwise the RF-only class remains and the Match cell lists the best candidate plus up to three alternatives within that eight-point ambiguity margin; its tooltip includes their scores and residuals. TLE matches are unavailable in builds where the SGP4 library was not found.

The same worker also evaluates the Moon using SDRangel's built-in lunar ephemeris. `Moon possible` is true only when the Moon is above the transmitter and receiver horizons and inside both configured antenna beams at the detection midpoint. The table then shows the expected bistatic center Doppler offset and its change over the detected sweep, calculated from the changing transmitter-Moon-receiver path length at the recording time. A sufficiently close, unambiguous Doppler fit is identified as `Moon`; the prediction remains visible even when another moving target is a better match.

## Detection

Detection operates on a two-dimensional spectrogram image rather than per-frame heuristics. Frames of about 125 ms (clamped to 32–256 samples, hopping every quarter frame) are zero-padded to twice the frame length before the FFT, giving finer frequency bins than the frame alone would allow. Columns accumulate in a rolling 20-second analysis window, and the per-bin noise floor is the median power across that window: a median is non-adaptive over the window, so a meteor's faint decay tail cannot raise the floor and mask itself. The excess-over-floor image is segmented by hysteresis: pixels at least 11 dB above the floor seed components (isolated specks are discarded), which then grow through all connected pixels at least 6.5 dB above the floor, capturing faint tails. A short vertical morphological close heals single-bin splits, and temporal fragments that continue one another's frequency drift within a tight envelope are linked into a single event.

A blob is accepted as a meteor when its integrated power — the sum of excess above the grow threshold over all its pixels — reaches the sensitivity threshold (the **Sens** setting; lower is more sensitive), its brightest pixel is at least 16 dB above the floor (faint spread noise can accumulate score over area but never gets bright), it does not occupy most of the band in any column (broadband impulses), and its frequency track is not an elongated, highly linear drift (satellite sweeps; meteor tracks are vertical or irregular). There is no machine-learned classifier: acceptance is one calibrated threshold plus these three vetoes. The defaults were calibrated against several hundred manually reviewed detections.

The rolling window is re-analysed every half second and a blob is reported as soon as it is provably complete — its last column is far enough behind the live edge that no later column could extend it — so detections appear on the display about a second after the echo ends. Reported times and durations include the STFT frame support on both ends. The frequency box hugs the bright core of the blob rather than its faint skirt, deliberately, following visual review of box quality. Frequency drift is the fitted slope of the blob's frequency track multiplied by its duration. The maximum duration setting clips longer reports and marks them truncated; events longer than roughly the analysis window cannot be measured intact.

## Satellite sweeps

Blobs rejected by the sweep veto are not discarded: they are consolidated along their common Doppler drift line into a single satellite detection per pass. Each active sweep maintains a running least-squares drift line; fragments join when the line predicts their frequency within tolerance. Faint below-threshold linear segments that continue an established line extend it, and when a pass completes, the detector walks the fitted line outward through chained faint dashes to cover the dashed leading and trailing parts of the track. Two consolidated sweeps that continue one another — a pass whose fast, faint mid-section was invisible — are merged rather than reported twice. An otherwise-accepted meteor-shaped blob lying on an established sweep's line within the sweep's own time span is counted as part of the sweep, not as a meteor.

## Regression harness

The standalone `meteor_demod_sink_test` harness replays stereo 16-bit IQ WAV recordings through the full channel:

```text
meteor_demod_sink_test --wav recording.wav --channel-sample-rate 3000 --details
```

Run without arguments it executes the regression fixtures in `test/tests`: each `name.csv` lists the expected detections for `name.wav` (time, duration, center frequency, span, drift, total power), compared within tight tolerances. A fixture named `name@3000.csv` runs the same recording at a 3000 Hz channel rate, so both calibrated rates are covered. Expectation CSVs may append a `required` column; optional rows describe genuinely ambiguous cases that may be absent but must match if present.

The harness accepts repeated `--tunable name=value` overrides for any `DetectorTunables` member (names drop the `m_` prefix), for example `--tunable blobScoreThreshold=100`, allowing operating points to be swept against recordings without rebuilding.

The detection algorithm is an exact port of the offline reference implementation in `test/detector2d/blobs.py`, verified by a golden-fixture parity test in `test/detector2d/cpp`. The research tooling under `test/detector2d` (offline detection, review rendering, and the browser-based labeler in `test/label_candidates.py`) was used to calibrate the acceptance threshold and box shaping and remains usable for future corpus work.
